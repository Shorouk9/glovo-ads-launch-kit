In May 2026 Glovo announced four new ad formats for brands. This is a plan to launch them in all 21 Glovo markets in about 28 weeks, while those markets are being split between two new owners, Uber and SSW Partners. 01 · The ride so far

Ads is the growth engine. The footprint is being reshaped underneath it.

Three things are true at the same time, and the launch plan has to hold all three.

Ads is outperforming everything else

Europe GMV grew 8.4% in Q2 2026, Quick Commerce 19%, and the high-margin AdTech line 32%. Group AdTech is approaching a €1.5bn run-rate at 3% of GMV, with a stated >4% long-term target. Every point of penetration is high-margin revenue — launch velocity is directly a P&L lever.

The product has a clear 2026 roadmap

Retail Media Day at Yellow Park (May 2026) introduced four new in-app opportunities for brands: audience segmentation, direct product links, video stories, and product cross-sell. Poland was named a key growth market. These formats are the launch scope of this kit.

Ownership is about to split

Uber's €13bn offer for Delivery Hero keeps 16 Glovo markets (17 announced, minus Bosnia, which closed in August), while Spain, Portugal, Poland, Romania and Moldova go to SSW Partners. Closing is expected to take ~a year. In Spain, Glovo cut back activity in around 60 towns this year. The map is being concentrated, and HQ sits in a market that is moving.

02 · Launch scope

Four formats, one bundle, launched wave by wave

Launching them as a bundle per market (rather than format by format across all markets) cuts the number of Legal, Finance and Commercial cycles by roughly 4×, and gives each market one "new Glovo Ads" story to sell.

1

Audience segmentation

Brands target by purchase behaviour and category affinity. Heaviest Legal and Data dependency — consent and cross-market audience rules differ by country.

2

Direct product links

Ad tap goes straight to the SKU. Lowest friction to launch; needs catalogue hygiene in grocery and retail partners.

3

Video stories

Full-screen in-app video. Design, localisation and creative-spec guidelines for agencies are the long poles.

4

Product cross-sell

Sponsored "add to basket" at checkout. Highest expected ROAS; depends on Q-commerce depth in the market.

What "launched" means

A format counts as launched in a market only when all five are true: (1) live in the app for 100% of eligible users, (2) bookable in Brands Ads Manager or the self-serve tool, (3) priced and billable in local currency, (4) at least one reference campaign measured end-to-end, (5) local Commercial team enabled with a one-pager and a deck. Partial states are tracked as "soft-live" and are never reported upward as done.

03 · Market tiering model

Which markets go first, and why

Each market is scored 1–5 on five dimensions. Readiness = ad maturity + Q-commerce momentum + advertiser depth + ops readiness − regulatory friction. Ownership then pulls ready SSW markets forward, so they launch well before the deal closes. Transition risk does not change the wave; it decides how much hand-over documentation a launch needs.

Wave 1

Revenue first, de-risked

Spain, Italy, Poland, Portugal, Romania. Four of five are SSW-bound, so Wave 1 launches are built hand-over-ready from day one: fully documented, no HQ-only dependencies. Italy anchors the Uber lane so Lane B is never a step behind.

Wave 2

Scale the Uber lane

Ukraine, Morocco, Kazakhstan, Nigeria, Kenya, Bulgaria, Croatia, Georgia. Strong Q-commerce momentum, growing FMCG presence, low transition risk. Partner Ads self-serve adoption is the lever as much as Brand Ads.

Wave 3

Complete the footprint

Serbia, Tunisia, Armenia, Kyrgyzstan, Montenegro, Moldova, Uganda, Côte d'Ivoire. Smaller advertiser bases; launched with a lighter "core three" (links, cross-sell, stories), segmentation gated on data volume.

How the three waves overlap

Wave 2 starts in week 9, while Wave 1 is in its last 4 weeks (launch and hypercare, run by local teams). By then the HQ teams — Product, Legal, Finance — have finished most of their Wave 1 work, so they move on to Wave 2 instead of waiting. Wave 3 starts in week 17 the same way. Result: 28 weeks in total instead of 36 if the waves ran one after another.

06 · Launching through the carve-out

Two lanes, one roadmap, zero paused launches

Nothing has been said publicly about how Glovo Ads itself will be run through the transaction. This is a framework to keep launching regardless of how the deal lands — and to make whatever gets handed over clean.

Lane A · SSW Partners
SpainPortugalPolandRomaniaMoldova

Profile: Glovo's most mature ad markets, deepest FMCG and agency relationships, home of HQ and Brands Ads Manager.

Risk: not readiness — continuity. Tooling, data pipelines and brand master agreements may need to be carved out or re-papered.

Rule: build every launch here as hand-over-ready. Full documentation, no HQ-only dependencies, no undocumented workarounds.

Lane B · Uber-retained
ItalyUkraineGeorgiaKazakhstanKyrgyzstanArmeniaBulgariaCroatiaSerbiaMontenegroMoroccoTunisiaNigeriaKenyaUgandaCôte d'Ivoire

Profile: Italy is mature; most others are earlier-stage with a heavier Partner Ads (self-serve SME) weighting. Africa is the fastest-growing region.

Risk: readiness and localisation, not ownership disruption. Longer-term question: convergence with Uber Eats' ad stack.

Rule: no Lane-A-only features. Lane B never becomes a second-class product before anyone has decided its future.

Governance

Structure

One global launch lead (this role). One market-cluster owner per lane. Product, Legal, Finance, Data as standing members of a fortnightly launch council.

Carve-out impact review

Monthly, with Legal and Finance. Every launch is checked for deal-dependency: does this contract, dataset or integration need to survive separation? Output feeds the RAID log under a dedicated "deal risk" category.

Escalation

Any blocker older than five working days goes on the exec one-pager with a named owner and a decision request. Lane conflicts escalate to the Partner Revenue lead, never resolved informally.

07 · First 30 days in the seat

Replace the assumptions, keep the structure

Days 1–10

Swap the illustrative tiering scores for internal data. Confirm the real format list and sequencing with Ads Product. Meet every market-cluster lead and the Legal, Finance and Data counterparts. Stand up the RAID log and the exec template.

Days 11–20

Run the first carve-out dependency review. Lock Wave 1 scope at G1. Publish the launch calendar to all local teams. Agree KPI definitions and pipeline ownership with Data.

Days 21–30

First fortnightly exec one-pager delivered. Wave 1 moving toward G2. Wave 2 kickoff scheduled. Any gap between this kit and reality written up as a change log — the plan should look different by then, and that is the point.

Terms specific to this kit

SSW / Uber markets
The 5 markets moving to SSW Partners (ES, PT, PL, RO, MD) and the 16 staying with Uber.
Lane
This kit's name for each of those two groups (A = SSW, B = Uber).
Close
When the Uber deal completes; expected ~a year after July 2026.
Hand-over-ready
Documented, with nothing only HQ can run, so a new owner can take it over.
Wave
A group of markets launching together over 12 weeks (5 / 8 / 8).
Bundle
All four new formats launched together in a market.
G1–G4
Gate checks at weeks 2, 5, 8, 10: scope lock, build ready, market ready, go / no-go.
Hypercare
The two weeks of close monitoring right after go-live.
Launched vs soft-live
Launched = live, bookable, billable, measured, team trained. Anything less is soft-live.
Readiness score
Ad maturity + Q-commerce + advertiser depth + ops readiness − regulatory friction (max 19).
Transition risk
1–5: how much a market's ads are tangled up in the split. Sets hand-over work, not the wave.
Reference advertiser
One of the first advertisers whose campaign proves a format works in a market.
